# Composition

Pass 6. These are Strunk's principles of composition (*The Elements of Style*, 1918), kept where they still help and read through the rulings in `SKILL.md`. They say what a good sentence does; the earlier passes say what a generated one does wrong. Apply them to sentences the earlier passes touched, and to any sentence that still reads stiff.

1. **One paragraph, one topic.** Open with the sentence that says what the paragraph is about. A new subject gets a new paragraph.
2. **Active voice when the actor matters.** "The job retries the request" beats "The request is retried". Keep the passive when the actor is unknown, beside the point, or the genre expects it (methods, legal text).
3. **Positive form.** Say what is, not what is not: "forgot" over "did not remember", "rejects empty input" over "does not accept empty input". This does not license "not X but Y" staging (patterns B1). It means state Y and drop X.
4. **Definite, specific, concrete.** Use a name, a number, a file, an endpoint or the person who acted, never a rarer synonym (Ruling 1). "The import fails on files over 2 GB" beats "the import has scalability limitations".
5. **Omit needless words.** Cut words that carry nothing, such as "the fact that", "in order to", "it is important to note", "there is… that". Never cut a claim, a qualifier the evidence needs, or a caveat (Ruling 3, fidelity).
6. **Avoid a run of loose sentences.** Several sentences of "X, and Y" or "X, which Y" in a row read as drift. Vary them by subordinating or splitting.
7. **Parallel form for parallel ideas.** Items in one list share grammatical form. Use the form only when the ideas really are parallel, and never pad or trim a list to fit it (Ruling 4).
8. **Keep related words together.** Put the subject near its verb and the modifier next to what it modifies: "the flag the deploy job reads", not "the flag, which, during deploy, the job reads".
9. **One tense in a summary.** A description of what a change does stays in the present.
10. **End on the word that matters.** The end of a sentence carries the stress. Put the new information there: "Retries stop after five attempts", not "After five attempts, retries are stopped, which is a hard limit".

Strunk's rules on English commas and possessives (his chapter I) are left out. They do not transfer to Polish, and modern English style guides cover them.
